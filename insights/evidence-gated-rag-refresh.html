Checking the evidence beat trusting confidence, but only when the checks were good

DAR-RB: when should a multi-step RAG agent re-retrieve, if it cannot afford to every time?

By Muzammal Hussain · 12 November 2026 · Research update

An agent that retrieves, reasons and hands its result to the next step can carry one stale or contradicted fact to the end. Re-retrieving at every step fixes that but costs the most. I compared four refresh policies in a simulator of 8-step workflows where evidence can be stale or contradicted and errors can carry forward. No policy sees whether evidence is really bad; each only sees noisy signals. Everything is synthetic, and the detector accuracies are my assumptions.

Key findings

  1. Evidence checks beat confidence: the gated policy finished 7.6 points more episodes with no wrong step than confidence-based refresh (paired 95% interval 6.6 to 8.7), with 1.29 fewer retrievals per episode.
  2. Refreshing every step still won on accuracy: 82.8% clean episodes against 57.4%, at 3.4 times the retrievals.
  3. The gate is only as good as its checks: with the stale-evidence check catching 70% instead of 80%, the gated policy fell to a tie with confidence refresh; at 60% it lost.

Setup

Each episode draws a stale-evidence rate (5 to 30%), a contradiction rate (2 to 20%) and a chance that an error carries into the next step (25 to 65%). 10,000 episodes over 5 seeds, and all four policies meet the same evidence at every step. Policies see a confidence score that overlaps between good and bad evidence, a freshness check (catches 80% of stale evidence, 5% false alarms), a cross-source check (70%, 5%) and a consistency check on carried errors (60%). A refresh fixes bad evidence 90% of the time.

The cross-source and consistency checks were lowered with it, staying 0.1 and 0.2 below.

What this means

If retrieval is cheap, refresh every step. If it is not, checking the evidence itself is a better trigger than the model's confidence, but only once the freshness and contradiction checks are measured and found to catch most bad evidence. A gate built on weak checks is worse than the simple confidence rule it replaces.

Limitations

The detector accuracies, fix rates and propagation rule are assumptions, not measurements. One workflow length and retrieval cost as a simple count. Nothing here has been checked against real agent traces yet.
