Confidence Inflation and Calibration

Published 24 October 2026 · Synthetic experiment

Confidence scores can become less reliable when reported confidence is systematically inflated. This experiment tests whether a simple correction estimated on separate calibration data improves held-out probabilistic predictions.

Key findings

  • Prediction accuracy was unchanged by the confidence correction, as expected for a confidence-only adjustment.
  • Raw Brier scores worsened as the simulated inflation level increased.
  • At inflation 0.25, the correction reduced Brier score in all five seeds, but the improvement was small.
  • Expected calibration error (ECE) did not improve uniformly across all settings.

Experimental design

The synthetic study used five seeds, three simulated agents with nominal skills 0.80, 0.70 and 0.62, a calibration sample of 1,000 cases and a held-out test sample of 2,000 cases. Inflation levels were 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.15, 0.20 and 0.25.

For each run, a simple additive correction was estimated from the calibration sample as the mean gap between reported confidence and correctness, then evaluated on held-out scenarios. Metrics were accuracy, Brier score and 10-bin ECE.

Mean accuracy across the reported settings was approximately 79.35% and did not change between raw and corrected confidence for a given run. The Brier-score gains at the highest inflation level were modest. ECE results varied by setting, so the correction should not be treated as a guaranteed calibration improvement.

Limitations

This is a synthetic proof-of-concept, not evidence that real agents share a constant additive confidence bias. The correction is deliberately simple and may fail under distribution shift, non-uniform errors or different confidence-generation processes. Calibration should be evaluated on representative held-out data.

Reproducibility

Experiment code and data: confidence-inflation experiment repository.

Run with python3 run_experiment.py --seeds 10; run tests with python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v. The experiment uses Python's standard library only.

Conclusion

Confidence correction can improve probabilistic scoring under controlled inflation, but the observed gains are small and ECE is not consistently improved. Measure calibration on held-out data before using corrected confidence in consequential decisions.
